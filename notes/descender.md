# Descender

The part of a letter that hangs below the [[baseline]], as in `g`, `j`, `p`, `q`, and `y`.

The descender line marks how low these letters reach.

Every line of text keeps room for descenders, whether or not the words in it have any. This is a common reason for text that looks off-centre. A label such as "Save" has no descenders, so the space kept for them sits empty below the word, and the word looks too high in its button. The fix is [[optical-alignment|optical alignment]]: a little less [[padding]] below, or a little more above.

Descenders also explain why a row of words can look uneven when their baselines are in fact level. A word with a tail seems to sit lower than its neighbour without one.

Underlines cross descenders. Browsers leave gaps in the underline around them by default.
