# Laws of UX

A website by Jon Yablonski that collects about thirty principles of interface design, one page each: [lawsofux.com](https://lawsofux.com/).

It describes itself as "a collection of best practices that designers can consider when building user interfaces". Each law has a short definition, a few takeaways, and its origin. Most come from psychology, and many are named after the person who described them, such as Fitts's Law and Hick's Law.

It is the closest well-known relative of this collection: short, one idea per page, and written to be looked up. The difference is the subject. Laws of UX is mostly about how people perceive and decide. This collection is mostly about the visual details of a screen.

Where the two overlap is grouping. Its Law of Proximity, Law of Common Region, and Law of Similarity are the [[gestalt-principles|Gestalt principles]] covered here under [[proximity]], [[common-region|common region]], and [[similarity]].

A caution about the word "law". These are observations and rules of thumb with varying amounts of evidence behind them, not rules that hold every time.
