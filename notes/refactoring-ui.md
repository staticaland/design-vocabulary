# Refactoring UI

A book by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger, the creators of Tailwind CSS, on making interfaces look good without being a designer: [refactoringui.com](https://www.refactoringui.com/).

Its tagline is "Make your ideas look awesome, without relying on a designer." It is written for developers, and it works through concrete tactics instead of theory. Each tip shows a before and an after.

Of all the sources here, it covers the most of the same ground as these notes:

- **"Hierarchy is Everything":** [[visual-hierarchy|visual hierarchy]] built from size, [[font-weight|weight]], and colour, and the advice to de-emphasise the surroundings instead of making the important thing louder. See [[emphasis]].
- **"Layout and Spacing":** choosing distances from a [[spacing-scale|spacing scale]], and starting with more space than seems necessary.
- **"Designing Text":** a fixed [[type-scale|type scale]] and a small set of weights.
- **"Working with Color":** building a [[colour-palette|palette]] of neutrals, a primary colour, and accents, each in several shades.

Its central habit is the one behind [[design-tokens|design tokens]]: decide a small set of values in advance, then choose from the set, so that no value is picked by eye on the spot.

The book is paid, and sold as a PDF with video tutorials.
