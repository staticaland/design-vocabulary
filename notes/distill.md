# Distill

A steering word: asking for a design to be reduced to its essentials by removing whatever does not earn its place.

It is the answer to clutter: too many buttons, repeated information, decoration that serves no purpose, everything shown at once. Where [quieter](quieter.md) softens what is there, distilling takes things away.

It starts from a question, not from the design: what is the one thing this screen is for? Everything is then judged against the answer. Each element is kept, combined with another, hidden until needed, or removed.

Common moves:

- **One primary action** per view, with the rest clearly secondary or tucked away.
- **Progressive disclosure:** showing only what is needed now, with detail available on request, for example behind "Advanced settings" or a "Show more" link.
- **Removing redundancy:** saying something once, and dropping a heading that repeats the sentence beneath it.
- **Flattening:** removing containers that add nothing. See [nesting](nesting.md) and [card](card.md).
- **Fewer styles:** a smaller [colour palette](colour-palette.md), and fewer sizes and weights.

Distilling removes obstacles, not capability. It goes wrong when it hides something users need in order to decide, removes labels until the interface becomes a puzzle, or makes a complex task look simpler than it is.
