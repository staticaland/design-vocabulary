# White space

The empty area between and around elements. Also called negative space. It does not have to be white, only free of content.

White space is not what is left over once the content is placed. It does work of its own: it separates groups (see [[proximity]]), it gives [[emphasis]] to whatever it surrounds, and it sets how dense or calm a screen feels. An element with room around it reads as important. The same element packed in among others reads as one of many.

Two scales are often told apart:

- **Micro white space:** the small spaces inside a component, such as [[padding]], the [[gap]] between a label and its field, and the [[line-height|space between lines]] of text. It mostly affects how easy something is to read.
- **Macro white space:** the large spaces between sections and around the content as a whole, such as page margins. It mostly affects structure and tone.

The usual failure is too little, from treating empty space as wasted space and filling it. A common tactic is to start with more space than seems necessary and take it away until the design feels right. The opposite failure exists too: so much space between related things that they stop reading as a group.

Amount is a matter of purpose, not correctness. A dense table for experts and a sparse landing page can both be right.
