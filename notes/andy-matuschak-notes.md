# Andy Matuschak's notes

The public working notes of the researcher Andy Matuschak, and the best-known example of linked notes on the web: [notes.andymatuschak.org](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/).

He describes them as written mostly for himself, as a thinking environment. There is no index and no navigation; a reader starts somewhere and follows links. Clicking a link opens the next note beside the current one, so a trail of notes builds up across the screen.

The notes describe their own method, which he calls evergreen notes: notes written to develop and stay useful over time. Each is about one idea, and the title is often a full claim, such as "Evergreen notes should be atomic", so that a link to a note reads as part of a sentence.

This collection borrows the outline of the method: one concept per note, a flat folder, and structure from links. It does not go as far. The titles here are terms, not claims, because the notes are for looking words up.

His subject is learning and tools for thought, not design, so this is a source for the format of the collection and not for its content.
