# Maggie Appleton's digital garden

The website of the designer Maggie Appleton, a well-known digital garden, and the home of a widely read essay on what a digital garden is: [maggieappleton.com](https://maggieappleton.com/).

A digital garden, in her words, is "a collection of evolving ideas that aren't strictly organised by their publication date". The contrast is with a blog, where posts are finished, dated, and listed newest first. Garden notes are linked by topic, published unfinished, and improved over time.

Her essay "A Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden" names six patterns: topography over timelines, continuous growth, imperfection and learning in public, playful and personal design, a mix of content types, and independent ownership. Her notes are marked by maturity, as seedling, budding, or evergreen.

This collection follows the first two patterns: it is arranged by links instead of dates, and it is meant to grow. It does not mark how finished each note is.

Unlike the other sources on format, much of her writing is about design.
