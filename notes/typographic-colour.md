# Typographic colour

The overall greyness of a block of text, seen as a texture instead of read as words. It has nothing to do with hue: black text on white still has a colour in this sense.

It comes from everything that controls how much ink sits in an area: the font, its [[font-weight|weight]] and [[font-size|size]], the spacing between letters, and the [[line-height|line height]]. A heavy font set tight is dark. A light font with open lines is pale.

The aim for running text is even colour. A paragraph should read as one flat grey, so that nothing draws the eye by accident. Typical things that break it:

- A second font inside the text, such as code, that is heavier or has a taller [[x-height]] and makes a dark spot.
- Bold used too often, which makes the paragraph blotchy.
- Justified text, where uneven gaps between words make pale streaks.

Uneven colour on purpose is [[emphasis]]. The point is that it should be chosen.

The [[squint-test|squint test]] is the way to see it: with the words blurred, only the texture is left.
