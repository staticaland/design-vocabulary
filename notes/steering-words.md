# Steering words

Words for the direction a design should move in, as opposed to words for the parts it is made of.

Most design vocabulary is nouns. [[baseline|Baseline]], [[padding]], and [[visual-weight|visual weight]] name things that can be pointed at and measured. They are for describing what is there, and for saying exactly what is wrong.

Steering words are a second kind. They are verbs and adjectives, and they describe a change: make this [[bolder]], make that [[quieter]], [[distill]] this screen. They are for the moment when nothing in particular is broken, and the design as a whole is too flat, too loud, or too crowded.

They matter because the usual phrases for that moment are vague. "Make it pop" and "clean it up" leave the other person, or a coding agent, to guess what should change. A steering word is useful when it has an agreed meaning: a known set of moves, and known ways it goes wrong. "Quieter" means less saturation, less weight, and more space. It does not mean grey and generic.

Each steering word works through the nouns. Bolder and quieter turn [[visual-weight|visual weight]] and [[emphasis]] up or down. Distilling removes elements so that the [[visual-hierarchy|visual hierarchy]] is clearer. Knowing the nouns is what makes it possible to check whether the steering did what was asked.

The steering words covered so far:

- [[bolder|Bolder]]: more confident and striking
- [[quieter|Quieter]]: less intense
- [[distill|Distill]]: reduced to the essentials
