# Ascender

The part of a lowercase letter that rises above the [[x-height]], as in `b`, `d`, `h`, `k`, and `l`.

The ascender line marks how high these letters reach. In most fonts it is slightly above the [[cap-height|cap height]], so a `d` is a little taller than an `H`.

Ascenders and [[descender|descenders]] give words their outline, which is part of why lowercase text is easier to read in bulk than text in capitals.

They are also what limits how tight a [[line-height|line height]] can go. When lines are set too close, the ascenders of one line touch the descenders of the line above.
